title: Design language
description: The window family. How Videorc looks, and the rules every videorc/ui component follows.
---

Videorc is a desktop app, not a web page. Every window sits on its own ground, panes are flush and split by hairlines, controls are desktop-sized, and color is information.

The reference is Videorc's logo: black metal with soft silver streaks, polished chrome on the controls you touch, and one LED-red accent. Every window shares one material and one set of rules, which is why the design is called the window family.

## Dark first, light as its twin

Dark is the reference. Light is the same structure with the light token column, never a separately designed theme: porcelain with graphite streaks and black-chrome controls, the dark chrome turned the other way around.

Every token has a dark and a light value. A component never checks which theme is on; it reads the tokens, and the `.dark` class swaps them.

## Color is information

The chrome is monochrome, and every neutral is truly neutral (chroma 0, no blue or warm cast). Saturated color appears only where it means something:

| Color | Token | Means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Red | `--live`, `--destructive` | Record, on air, failed, destructive. The logo's LED red |
| Green | `--success` | Healthy, connected |
| Amber | `--warning` | Needs attention |
| Blue | `--info` | Neutral news. Rare |
| Brand colors | `--color-platform-*` | Source and platform icons, the only large color on screen |

- Never tint a panel, a row or text for decoration.
- Never color a status word. The tone rides on an icon, a dot, a chip or an alert, and the word stays monochrome.
- There's no colored action accent. The primary action is chrome, not blue.

## The window and its coats

Every Videorc window is opaque and paints the theme's plain solid: #070707 in dark, #FAFAFA in light, the same on every platform. Chrome lives in the controls and the chrome rows, never behind them.

Each region paints a coat of the theme's solid tone. How much of the solid a region paints is its cover:

| Region          | Utility            | Cover, dark | Cover, light |
| --------------- | ------------------ | ----------- | ------------ |
| The window body | `bg-glass-window`  | 82%         | 86%          |
| The sidebar     | `bg-glass-sidebar` | 88%         | 92%          |
| Content panes   | `bg-glass-content` | 82%         | 86%          |

The body paints the lighter cover, and the sidebar and content panes add exactly the delta that brings them to theirs. The sidebar is the heavier region. On the solid window every region composites to the solid; the covers still decide the frost when something translucent sits behind.

- **Never stack a third coat.** Each region composites to exactly its cover. A coated card on a coated pane comes out darker than anything was tuned for.
- **Change covers, not coats.** The coats are derived from `--glass-cover-sidebar` and `--glass-cover-work`. See [Theme](/docs/concepts/theme).
- **Contrast is measured, not eyeballed.** Each text tier is checked over the solid and at Increase Contrast: primary at 7:1, secondary at 4.5:1, tertiary at 3:1.
- **No `backdrop-filter`, anywhere.** On an Electron window with a native material, a CSS backdrop blur shows in Chromium's own screenshots but never reaches the display, and in Videorc it once froze the compositor. A live blur also costs GPU time on every frame, which a recording app can't spare.

## Floating glass

What floats is flat and opaque: a tier of the window's solid tone with a 1 px rim and a drop shadow. No sheen, no highlight, no blur. Without a frost, any translucency only leaks: at 97% opacity, the white text under a hover card still reads through.

| Utility | For | Dark | Light |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `glass-float` | Menus, selects, popovers, hover cards, chart tooltips, toasts | About `#1D1D1D` | Porcelain |
| `glass-float-dialog` | Dialogs and the command palette | About `#161616` | Porcelain, with a deeper drop |
| `glass-float-tooltip` | Tooltips | The solid itself | Porcelain |

- Pair each with `border`: the utility sets the rim's color, and the element brings its width.
- One surface per float. A `Command` inside a [Dialog](/docs/components/dialog) stays transparent.
- Never `bg-popover` with a ring or a shadow. In this theme `--popover` follows `glass-float`, so a freshly added shadcn component still lands on the right tone.

## Chrome materials

The controls you touch shine, and nothing else does. Light is the crisp invert: black chrome on porcelain.

| Utility | What it is | Who wears it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `chrome-fill` | The polished primary fill | [Button](/docs/components/button) `default`, a checked checkbox, a switch that's on, progress, slider ranges |
| `chrome-plate` | Selected or active | The active segment of a [SegmentedControl](/docs/components/segmented-control), the active tab, a pressed toggle, the sidebar's current page |
| `chrome-bead`, `chrome-bead-ink` | Knobs and thumbs | Slider thumbs and the switch knob; `-ink` is the dark bead for a knob on a chrome track |
| `chrome-led` | A red LED | Button `record` only: Record and its stop button. `data-lit` makes it glow while a recording or livestream runs |

- **Only components wear materials.** A screen gets the shine through a component's variant, never by adding a class.
- **Everything else stays flat.** Panels, sections, rows, menus, dialogs, toasts and text never take a material or a gradient. No gradient text, no sheen on toolbars or panes.
- **States.** A fill lifts a little on hover and turns concave when pressed. Disabled is flat. Hover on a plated segment stays the flat `bg-accent`, because the plate means selected.
- **A lit LED only ever means record or live.** Delete stays `destructive`, never `record`.
- The materials write into Tailwind's shadow stack, so a `ring-*` focus ring still draws over them.

## Glass chips and tones

Badges, status pills, tags and key chips are small pieces of the window glass: a gradient fill, a 1 px rim, a top highlight and a soft drop. They never move and never blur.

| Utility | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `glass-chip` | A neutral chip: a [Badge](/docs/components/badge), a secondary [Button](/docs/components/button) |
| `glass-chip-tinted` | An emphasis chip: the tone tints the glass. Only for failed, on air and destructive |
| `glass-dot` | A status dot: the tone, a 2 px halo and a soft glow |
| `glass-keycap` | A key, not a tag: a brighter top edge and a dark bottom edge. [Kbd](/docs/components/kbd) wears it |

Color rides in one variable, `--chip-tone`, which a tone utility sets:

| Utility            | Means                         |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------- |
| `tone-success`     | Healthy, connected            |
| `tone-warning`     | Needs attention               |
| `tone-destructive` | Failed                        |
| `tone-live`        | On air, recording             |
| `tone-info`        | Neutral news                  |
| `tone-neutral`     | Idle: a gray dot with no glow |

A status chip keeps monochrome text and shows its tone in a 6 px dot. Only an emphasis chip tints the glass itself. Reach for [Badge](/docs/components/badge) rather than a hand-rolled pill.

## Three text tiers

| Utility | Tier | For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `text-foreground` | Primary | Titles, labels, values |
| `text-muted-foreground` | Secondary | Descriptions, metadata, placeholders, and every fact the user needs |
| `text-subtle` | Tertiary | Section labels, quiet hints, disabled text |

Three tiers, nothing else. Essential information never goes in `text-subtle`: a reason, a limit, a status or a value is secondary or primary. The tertiary tier passes AA on the solid, but holds only 3:1 over the ground's worst pixel.

No text is smaller than 11 px. The sizes are tokens: see [Desktop scale](/docs/concepts/desktop-scale#type).

## Radius tiers

| Utility         | Size  | For                      |
| --------------- | ----- | ------------------------ |
| `rounded-panel` | 12 px | Containers, dialogs      |
| `rounded-row`   | 8 px  | Rows, cards, tooltips    |
| `rounded-chip`  | 6 px  | Controls, key chips      |
| `rounded-tag`   | 7 px  | Tags                     |
| `rounded-lg`    | 10 px | Menus, selects, popovers |

Status chips are fully round. Window corners belong to the OS. There are no ad hoc radii.

## Motion

Fast and subtle: 100–150 ms, ease-out. Floating surfaces fade and scale from 0.98, and rows highlight instantly. Nothing bounces, chips never move, and a button doesn't shift when pressed. The ground is a still.

When the system asks to reduce motion, the theme collapses every CSS transition and animation. For motion CSS can't see, such as a script-driven scroll, use [useReducedMotion](/docs/hooks/use-reduced-motion).

## Keyboard first

Every primary action has a shortcut, and shows it as a key chip. Global hints live in the [status bar](/docs/primitives/status-bar), not on every surface.

- Write a shortcut once, as an Electron accelerator (`CmdOrCtrl+Shift+J`), and print it with [ShortcutKeys](/docs/components/shortcut-keys): ⇧⌘J on macOS, Ctrl+Shift+J on Windows and Linux. Never type ⌘ or "Cmd+" by hand.
- A global key never fires from a text field or from under an open dialog or menu. See [Modal open](/docs/hooks/modal-open).
- A [tooltip](/docs/components/tooltip) carries a control's name and its shortcut. A tooltip is never the only place a fact lives.
- The focus ring shows on keyboard focus only.

## Toast discipline

A toast is for news the interface doesn't already show. When in doubt, don't toast: someone mid-task reads every popup as an interruption.

- **No success toast for a change the user just watched happen.** In Videorc, switching a scene, a layout or a source never toasts: the stage is the confirmation.
- **Type by meaning.** The type colors the 16 px icon only, never the panel, its rim or its text. The theme makes [Sonner](/docs/components/sonner) toasts neutral glass even with `richColors`.

| Type | When |
| --- | --- |
| Error | It failed, and needs you |
| Warning | It can't happen now, or it's degraded. A refusal is a warning, never an error |
| Info | Neutral news, or a step to take somewhere else |
| Success | Something finished out of view: a recording saved, an import done, an account connected |

- The title is the outcome, with no period: "MP4 ready", "Couldn't import the image". The description is whole sentences. Add at most one action, named by its verb: "Retry", "Show in Finder".
- About 4 seconds for one line, 8 or more with a description, and until it's dismissed when it has an action.
- One id per kind of toast, so a newer one replaces the older and the same news never stacks.

## One primitive per job

Each job has one shared primitive. Screens compose them; they don't restyle them. If a primitive lacks something, extend it a little rather than hand-roll a competitor.

| Need | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Page sections | [PanelSection](/docs/primitives/panel-section) |
| A list of like things | A `GroupedList` of [ListRow](/docs/primitives/list-row)s |
| Global state and hints | [StatusBar](/docs/primitives/status-bar) |
| A destructive confirmation | [ConfirmDialog](/docs/primitives/confirm-dialog) |
| Any other modal | [Dialog](/docs/components/dialog) |
| A choice of 2 to 4 values | [SegmentedControl](/docs/components/segmented-control) |
| Per-item actions | [DropdownMenu](/docs/components/dropdown-menu) |
| Why a control is disabled | [DisabledReason](/docs/components/disabled-reason) |
| A shortcut | [ShortcutKeys](/docs/components/shortcut-keys) |
| Status, tags and counts | [Badge](/docs/components/badge) |
| News | [Sonner](/docs/components/sonner) |

## Words

The copy on these surfaces follows a few rules:

- Sentence case everywhere: "Save local recording". Capitalize names only.
- Plain, short and specific. Say what happened, then what to do. Use contractions and American English, and no em dashes.
- One "…" character, never three dots. It goes on a command that asks for more ("Rename…") and on a pending label ("Saving…").
- "Choose", not "Select". Delete destroys; Remove takes something out of a list.
- A disabled control always says why.
- An error names what failed ("Couldn't import the image"), then why, then one fix.
